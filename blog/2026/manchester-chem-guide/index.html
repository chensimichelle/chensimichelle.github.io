UK · Chemistry · Chemical Engineering · Materials Science & Engineering · A-Level Applicant Guide · 2027 Entry

University of Manchester

This guide covers 6 undergraduate programmes across 3 departments — Chemistry (BSc/MChem), Chemical Engineering (BEng/MEng) and Materials Science & Engineering (BSc/MEng) — at the University of Manchester. Manchester is the historic birthplace of chemical engineering as a discipline and one of Europe's largest centres for materials research. Written for students applying with A-levels from international schools for 2027 entry.

Key differentiators: Chemistry is not routinely interviewed; Chemical Engineering invites every eligible UK-based applicant to a UCAS Interview Day; Materials invites eligible applicants to a Virtual Visit Day that includes an interview. Entry grades range from AAB (Materials BSc) up to A*AA (MChem Chemistry). All three departments sit within the Faculty of Science and Engineering.

Why there's no success-rate column filled in: Since at least 2020, Manchester has maintained a University-wide policy of refusing to release undergraduate application/offer/intake figures at individual-course level under FOI Section 43(2) (Commercial Interests), on the grounds that per-course figures would reveal "market sensitive information" to competitor universities. The only confirmed exceptions are Medicine and Dentistry, which fall outside this guide. For Chemistry, Chemical Engineering and Materials there simply isn't a published number to show — this reflects Manchester's standing policy, not a gap in research.
IELTS reading tip: the two numbers in the table are "overall / minimum per component." Chemical Engineering is the strictest — it requires 6.5 in every sub-skill, not just overall, while Chemistry and Materials only require 6.0 in each sub-skill alongside a 6.5 overall.

Which Programme Suits You?

Find your best fit based on your interests and strengths

"I love atoms, molecules and chemical reactions for their own sake"
→ Chemistry (BSc/MChem)
Also consider: Chemical Engineering (applied, process-scale)
"I want to design industrial or pharmaceutical manufacturing processes"
→ Chemical Engineering
Also consider: Chemistry (if fundamentals-first)
"I'm fascinated by metals, polymers, ceramics or nanomaterials like graphene"
→ Materials Science & Engineering
Also consider: Chemistry (materials chemistry option unit)
"I want a research career or PhD in the chemical sciences"
→ MChem Chemistry
Also consider: MEng Chemical Engineering
"I want professional accreditation and a fast route into industry"
→ BEng Chemical Engineering or BSc Materials
Also consider: the 4-year MEng/MChem if aiming for Chartered status
"I don't have Chemistry at A-level but do have Maths and Physics"
→ Materials Science & Engineering
Also consider: Chemical Engineering (Maths + Physics also accepted)
Important: Chemistry offers a common BSc/MChem pathway — you can transfer between the two up to Year 3, so applying for the MChem keeps both routes open. Chemical Engineering and Materials both offer a direct BEng/BSc route and a 4-year MEng route with the same first-year grade requirement in Chemical Engineering's case, so choose based on whether you want the extra specialisation year, not just entry difficulty.

How to Apply

UCAS Application Process

Step-by-step for 2027 entry · All six programmes share the January UCAS equal-consideration deadline

A-Level student applies →
2026
Register on UCAS Hub — Create your account at ucas.com. You may apply to more than one Manchester course, but not normally more than one first-year course within the same department.
2026
Write your personal statement — One statement for all UCAS choices. Name specific topics, reactions, materials or processes that excite you and link them to your A-level subjects.
Jan 2027
Submit UCAS application — Equal consideration deadline. Include school-submitted predicted grades; non-UK schools without a predicted-grades policy should have this explained by the referee.
Nov 2026 – Mar/Apr 2027
Interviews where applicable — Chemical Engineering (UCAS Interview Day) and Materials (Virtual Visit Day) applicants are invited during this window. Chemistry applicants are not routinely interviewed.
Jan–Apr 2027
Decisions issued — Offers are conditional on final A-level grades and subjects specified in your offer letter.
Aug 2027
A-level results & confirmation — Meet your conditional offer to secure your place. Grades from resits are considered but may need extra supporting information.

Personal Statement Do's & Don'ts

One statement for all UCAS choices · Interviewers (ChemEng/Materials) will read it before your interview

DO
  • Name specific reactions, materials, or processes that excite you
  • Show realistic understanding of what the course involves day-to-day
  • Link your A-level subjects directly to the degree content
  • Mention any relevant projects, reading, or lab experience
  • Prepare for interview if applying to Chemical Engineering or Materials
DON'T
  • Apply to more than one first-year course in the same department
  • Open with generic sentences ("Since childhood I have loved science…")
  • Assume Chemistry will interview you the way Chemical Engineering does
  • Forget the GCSE Maths grade 6/B requirement for Chemistry
  • Confuse the BSc and MEng/MChem grade requirements when self-predicting

Common Application Mistakes

Applying to more than one Chemistry course in the same first year
Pick one — internal transfer between BSc and MChem is possible up to Year 3
Assuming Materials only needs Chemistry
It needs any 2 of Maths, Physics, Chemistry — check which two you hold
Not preparing at all for the Chemical Engineering UCAS Interview Day
It's informal, but be ready to discuss your personal statement in depth
Overlooking the Chemical Engineering IELTS sub-skill rule
Every sub-skill must be 6.5+ here, unlike Chemistry or Materials (6.0 each)
Missing the GCSE Maths grade 7/A rule for Materials applicants without A-level Maths
Check this early if you're not taking A-level Mathematics
Letting IELTS expire before the course start date
Valid 2 years only — plan your test date well ahead
