King's College London - Chemistry, Natural Sciences, Biomedical Engineering & Pharmacy Guide

Created on July 20, 2026

2026   ·   uk   program introduction

UK · Chemistry · Natural Sciences · Biomedical Engineering · Pharmacy · A-Level Applicant Guide · 2026–27 Entry

King's College London

This guide covers 9 degree routes across 5 programme families — Chemistry, Chemistry with Biomedicine, Natural Sciences, Biomedical Engineering, and Pharmacy — at King's College London, ranked #37 in the world / #6 in the UK (QS World University Rankings 2027). Written for students applying with A-levels from international schools for 2026–27 entry.

Important scope note: King's has no Chemical Engineering degree — this is confirmed against KCL's official department listings and is a genuine structural gap versus institutions like UCL, not a data-access limitation. Biomedical Engineering is the closest engineering-adjacent route. Eight of the nine programmes require no interview or admissions test — selection is by A-level grades and personal statement through UCAS alone. Pharmacy MPharm is the one exception: it carries a mandatory 30-minute recorded interview and a different English language band, flagged throughout this guide.

Eight programmes, no interview — one exception: Chemistry, Chemistry with Biomedicine, Natural Sciences and Biomedical Engineering all make offers on UCAS application content alone — grades and personal statement, no interview or admissions test. Pharmacy MPharm breaks this pattern: it requires a mandatory 30-minute recorded interview on Microsoft Teams assessing communication, numeracy, values and motivation, plus a criminal-conviction disclosure/DBS check and an 18-by-1-January age requirement (for placement eligibility). If you're applying to Pharmacy alongside any of the other eight, budget real interview-prep time specifically for it.

Programme Introductions

Chemistry
BSc · 3 years / MSci · 4 years · Department of Chemistry · UCAS: F100 / F103
No InterviewRSC Accredited
What is it?

Rigorous training in the core principles of chemistry — physical, organic and inorganic — plus hands-on laboratory skills, from how medicines work to developing sustainable solutions. King's own framing explicitly points graduates toward the chemical, biotech and pharmaceutical industries, alongside tech, finance and law. Ranked 8th in the UK for Chemistry (THE/QS 2025 subject rankings).

What you'll study
  • Physical, organic & inorganic chemistry
  • Integrated laboratory course & open-ended project
  • Mathematics for Chemists (for students without A-level Maths)
  • MSci only: substantial 4th-year research project — in the Department, King's Health Faculties, or the Francis Crick Institute
Career paths
  • Research scientist (pharma / academia)
  • Chemical analyst
  • Biotech & nanotechnology roles
  • PhD / postgraduate research

Chemistry with Biomedicine
BSc · 3 years / MSci · 4 years · Department of Chemistry · UCAS: FC17 / F1C7
No InterviewRSC Accredited
What is it?

King's is the only London university — and one of very few in the UK — to offer a dedicated Chemistry with Biomedicine degree. It layers the same rigorous physical/organic/inorganic chemistry core with biomedicine content: the chemistry of disease, drug design and delivery, and the chemistry of living systems, drawing on King's world-class Health Faculties and teaching hospitals.

What you'll study
  • Physical, organic & inorganic chemistry core
  • Chemistry of disease, drug design & delivery
  • Integrated laboratory course & open-ended project
  • MSci only: 4th-year research project across Chemistry, Health Faculties/hospitals, or the Francis Crick Institute
Career paths
  • Biomedical engineering & chemical biology
  • Biotech & biomedical research
  • Pharmaceutical industry
  • PhD / postgraduate research

Natural Sciences
BSc · 3 years / MSci · 4 years · Faculty of Natural, Mathematical & Engineering Sciences · UCAS: CFG0 / CFG2
No Interview
What is it?

An interdisciplinary degree spanning biology, chemistry, mathematics and physics, for students who don't want to commit to one single-subject department. Bespoke modules explore the interconnections between the sciences, with a strong emphasis on computational and mathematical skills — coding, simulation, and machine learning/AI tools — plus an entrepreneurship-focused final-year project.

What you'll study
  • Year 1: core biology, chemistry, mathematics & physics foundations
  • Years 2–3: advanced maths, Machine Learning/AI, bespoke interdisciplinary modules
  • Optional Year Abroad between Years 2 and 3
  • Final-year research project with an employability/entrepreneurship module
Career paths
  • Emerging & interdisciplinary science industries
  • Data science / computational science
  • Scientific consultancy
  • PhD / postgraduate research

Biomedical Engineering
BEng · 3 years / MEng · 4 years · School of Biomedical Engineering & Imaging Sciences · UCAS: H160 / H161
No InterviewCEng Accredited
What is it?

Trains the next generation of biomedical engineers for healthcare research and development, with graduates going on to specialise in medical engineering, medical physics, or further study in Medicine. Based in a clinical setting at St Thomas' Hospital — a UK centre of excellence for medical imaging, with access to on-site MRI and PET facilities in later years.

What you'll study — Year 1 (no optional modules)
  • Mathematics & Mechanics for Biomedical Engineering
  • Physics for Biomedical Engineering
  • Introduction to Human Anatomy & Physiology
  • Introduction to Electrical & Electronic Engineering, Computer Programming, CAD, Computational Statistics
Career paths
  • Clinical / medical engineering
  • Medical devices R&D
  • Medical physics
  • Further study in Medicine

Pharmacy
MPharm · 4 years · Teaching Department of Pharmacy · UCAS: B230
Interview RequiredGPhC Accredited
What is it?

The only UK qualification leading to professional registration as a pharmacist. An integrated four-year course combining pharmaceutical science with clinical pharmacy practice, taught alongside nursing, medical, dental and midwifery students, with placements at Guy's, King's College Hospital and St Thomas'. Reaccredited by the General Pharmaceutical Council for 6 years in February 2025 with no conditions. Ranked 7th in Europe for Pharmacy & Pharmacology (QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026).

What you'll study
  • Year 1: underpinning scientific principles + intro to pharmacy practice
  • Years 2–3: disease, drugs & clinical uses of medicines; pharmacist-specific science
  • Year 4: clinical decision-making, emerging therapeutics, substantial research project
  • Increasing clinical placement time each year; optional Semester 1 study abroad in Year 4
Career paths
  • Registered pharmacist — hospital or community
  • GP practices & care homes
  • Pharmaceutical industry
  • Clinical research

How to Apply

UCAS Application Process

Step-by-step for 2026–27 entry · Shared timeline for all nine routes — Pharmacy adds an interview stage

A-Level student applies →
May 2026
Register on UCAS Hub — Registration opens 12 May 2026. Choose up to 5 UCAS choices; you can mix King's programmes with other universities.
1 Sep 2026
Submissions open — Completed UCAS applications can be submitted from 1 September 2026.
Autumn 2026
Write your personal statement — One statement for all 5 UCAS choices. For the 8 no-interview programmes this is doing most of the persuasive work; for Pharmacy, King's says it can consider a Medicine/Dentistry-style statement but still wants pharmacy-specific commitment.
13 Jan 2027
Submit UCAS application — 18:00 UK time equal-consideration deadline for all nine programmes in this guide (none carry the earlier 15 October Oxbridge/Medicine/Dentistry deadline).
Jan–Mar 2027
Pharmacy applicants only: interview invitations — a 30-minute recorded interview on Microsoft Teams, covering motivation, numeracy, values/judgement and a situational scenario. You'll need to show photo ID on camera at the start.
Jan–May 2027
Decisions issued — Chemistry, Chemistry with Biomedicine, Natural Sciences and Biomedical Engineering are assessed on grades and personal statement alone; Pharmacy decisions also fold in interview performance.
Aug 2027
A-level results & confirm — Meet your conditional offer. Resits are accepted by King's for these programmes (check the specific course page, as this can vary). Pharmacy offer-holders also complete Occupational Health Clearance, usually notified end of May/early June.

Personal Statement Do's & Don'ts

One statement for all 5 choices · Carries extra weight for the 8 programmes with no interview

DO
  • Name specific chemistry topics, biomedical applications, or engineering problems that excite you
  • Link your A-level subjects directly to the degree content
  • Show a clear commitment to pharmacy specifically if applying to Pharmacy — not just "science" in general
  • Mention relevant lab work, EPQ-adjacent projects, or reading — note King's does not consider the EPQ itself for entry
  • Write for your subject — one statement must serve all 5 UCAS choices
DON'T
  • Assume an interview will "fix" a weak statement for the 8 non-Pharmacy programmes — there isn't one
  • Take A-level General Studies, Critical Thinking, Thinking Skills or Global Perspectives as one of your three A-levels — King's does not accept these
  • Confuse Chemistry (F100/F103) with Chemistry with Biomedicine (FC17/F1C7) UCAS codes
  • Apply to Biomedical Engineering or Natural Sciences assuming a science A-level substitutes for Mathematics — Maths A is compulsory for both
  • Under-prepare for the Pharmacy interview by treating it like a formality — King's explicitly reviews the recording for consistency

Common Application Mistakes

Applying to Biomedical Engineering or Natural Sciences without A-level Mathematics
Maths at grade A is a fixed requirement for both — the second subject slot is what varies
Assuming Chemical Engineering exists at King's
It doesn't — Biomedical Engineering is the closest engineering-adjacent route; consider UCL or Imperial if Chemical Engineering specifically is the goal
Applying to Pharmacy without checking the age requirement
You must be 18 by 1 January of Year 1 (placement eligibility) — if you won't be, apply the following cycle
Not disclosing spent/unspent convictions on the Pharmacy UCAS application
UCAS requires this disclosure for Pharmacy specifically; offers are conditional on a satisfactory DBS check
Missing the practical endorsement in science A-levels (England, linear specs)
Required for Chemistry, Chemistry with Biomedicine, Natural Sciences and Biomedical Engineering — check with your school early
Assuming Pharmacy uses the same IELTS threshold as the other 8 programmes
Pharmacy sits at Band B (7.0 overall / 6.5 per skill) — the other eight sit at Band D (6.5/6.0)
